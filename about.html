The Craft

Block printing is not a technique. It is a conversation — between the printer, the block, the cloth, and the dye. Each stamp is a deliberate act; each repeat carries a breath of imperfection that no machine can replicate.

Every Ekaika piece begins as pure cotton cloth, washed and prepared to receive dye evenly. A hand-carved wooden block is dipped in colour and pressed into the fabric, stamp by stamp, until a pattern that started as a single motif becomes a field. Because it is done by hand and by eye, no two lengths of cloth are ever quite identical — and we think that's the point.

Colours come from natural and low-impact sources — indigo, pomegranate rind, iron-rust — mixed fresh in small batches. The cloth is then washed in running water and dried under open sky, which is what gives block-printed cotton its particular, lived-in softness.

The Artisans

The Artisans

We work with artisan families whose craft stretches back generations, printing in small workshops where the block table has often been passed down from parent to child. When you bring an Ekaika piece home, you carry a small part of that lineage with you.

We're building out this page with more of their stories, workshop by workshop — check back, or follow along on Instagram where we share the process as it happens.

Sustainability

Sustainability

Slow cloth is, by nature, a slower way of making things. We work in pure cotton, favour natural and low-impact dyes over synthetic ones, and print to order rather than stockpiling — which means less waste at every step between block and bed.

This part of our story is still being written as we formalise our sourcing and impact reporting. We'd rather share real detail as it's verified than make claims ahead of it.
